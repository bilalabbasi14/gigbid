# Data Card

Every dataset GigBid is developed and evaluated on, where it came from, what is real, what is
synthetic, and what that means for the numbers in the report.

## 1. Job posts (real, public)

| Source | Rows used | Licence | Role in GigBid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kaggle `asaniczka/upwork-job-postings-dataset-2024-50k-records` (53,058 posts, Feb 2024) | 790 | ODC Attribution (ODC-By) | GigSim job pool; pricing statistics |
| Kaggle `ahmedmyalo/upwork-freelance-jobs-60k` (63,950 posts, 2023) | 1,310 | Apache-2.0 | GigSim job pool; client history (spend, verification, rating, Connects cost, applicant counts) |

**Preprocessing** (`data/scripts/prepare_jobs.py`): HTML unescaping; splitting the 50k rows'
embedded `Budget / Category / Skills` tail out of the description; price sanity bounds
(fixed USD 20 to 50,000, hourly USD 3 to 500); keyword classification into the three persona
categories (`ai_ml`, `fullstack`, `data_analyst`) requiring at least two category signals and no
exclusion term (WordPress themes, 3D, logo, video); SHA-1 fingerprint de-duplication on title and
the first 300 characters; 700 jobs sampled per persona from the clearly on-topic set. Output:
`gigsim/seed/jobs.jsonl.gz` (2,100 jobs) and `gigsim/seed/market_stats.json` (p10 to p90 price
quantiles per persona and payment type).

**Known limitations.** Posts were collected in 2023 and 2024, so prices skew older than today's
market. The 50k rows carry no client fields, so their clients receive synthetic histories (marked
below). Country and spend distributions reflect Upwork's scrape-time population, not any target
market. Both datasets were collected by their Kaggle authors; GigBid does not scrape anything.

## 2. Adversarial posts (synthetic, template-based)

`data/scripts/inject_adversarial.py` generates 153 posts, deterministic under a seed:

| Kind | Count | How | Gold label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scams | 45 | five templates: free trial work, off-platform contact and payment, pay-to-apply, phishing portal, too-good-to-be-true pay | `scam=true`, `flags`, `gold_decision=skip` |
| Prompt injections | 72 | an injection sentence inserted at the start, middle or end of a real post; 8 families match `data/policy_pack/injection_patterns.yaml`, 4 "subtle" families deliberately do not | `injection=true`, `injection_ids` |
| Duplicates | 36 | a real post re-posted with a changed title prefix and a suffix line | `duplicate_of` |

Gold labels are stored in `evals/datasets/adversarial_gold.jsonl` and are never served by GigSim.

## 3. Simulated clients (synthetic, derived)

Clients are reconstructed from the 60k rows' public client fields (country, payment verified,
total spent, rating, review count); rows that share those fields are treated as one client. Posts
without client fields receive a seeded synthetic history. Each client also receives **hidden
preferences** (must-have skills drawn from the post, a budget ceiling, dislike of generic
openings, concision, whether screening answers matter) that drive the decision engine in
`gigsim/clients.py`. In LLM mode an open model (`qwen3:4b-instruct` by default) also reads each
proposal as that client and its interest is blended with the rule score. Hidden preferences are
never exposed through the API. Outcome metrics (reply rate, hire rate) therefore measure how
simulated clients with these preferences react, not real human behaviour; see the report for the
caveat.

## 4. Persona corpora (synthetic, LLM-generated)

The starter kit did not include the three persona corpora, so they are generated by
`data/scripts/gen_personas.py` with open models via Groq (gpt-oss-120b; the last data-analyst proposal batches used Qwen3.8 27B after the larger model's daily quota ran out) from a fixed seed and
committed under `data/personas/<persona>/`. Each persona has (counts for ai_ml / fullstack / data_analyst):

| Artefact | Count | Purpose for RAG |
|---|---|---|
| `projects.json` | 15 projects | ground truth catalogue every other artefact derives from |
| `case_studies/*.md` | 15 | prose view; three writing styles so the same project reads differently |
| `repos/<slug>/` | 8 / 8 / 14 | README plus source files and a manifest; the key skill is often only implied by the code |
| `cv.md`, `cv.pdf` | 1 | PDF ingestion path |
| `reviews.jsonl` | 12 | client voice and industries |
| `proposals.jsonl` | 60 / 54 / 60 | past proposals labelled `won`, `replied` or `ignored`, each citing project ids and tied to a real job from the pool |

Persona names, clients, metrics and code are fictional. Proposal outcome labels are assigned at
generation time by instructing the model to write in a winning, mediocre or weak style, so they
encode writing quality rather than real client decisions. The corpora are smaller than the
assignment's description of the instructor set (15 to 25 case studies, about 150 proposals) by
design; the generator takes `--projects` and `--proposals` to scale them up, and the instructor
corpora replace this folder unchanged in code when received.

The hidden test set is instructor-owned and never enters the repository.

## 6. Handling and privacy

Raw Kaggle downloads live in `data/raw/` (git-ignored). Only derived, attributed subsets are
committed. No real freelancer data is used; live-mode data from a real Upwork account is never
committed. Job posts and client messages are treated as untrusted input throughout the system.
