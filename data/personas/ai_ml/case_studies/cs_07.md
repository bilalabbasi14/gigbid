# Legal Contract Review Bot (2023 – Legal Startup)

## Context
The client, a fast‑growing legal‑tech startup, was building a platform to help in‑house counsel and law firms triage large volumes of contracts. Their product needed an automated assistant that could surface risky clauses instantly, allowing lawyers to focus on higher‑value work. The engagement lasted **9 weeks** in early 2023, with Priya Raman serving as **Lead NLP Engineer**.

## Challenge
Lawyers were manually reviewing thousands of contracts, a process that took days per batch and often missed subtle risk indicators. The specific challenges were:

| Issue | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| Unstructured contract text | Inconsistent clause formatting made keyword search unreliable |
| Large corpus (≈ 12 k contracts, ~ 3 M clauses) | Indexing and retrieval had to be scalable |
| Need for real‑time answers | Queries had to return in **< 1 s** to fit the user workflow |
| High precision required | False positives erode trust; target precision **≥ 0.94** for risk identification |

## Approach
Priya led the design of a Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline:

1. **Data preprocessing** – Used spaCy 3.5 for clause segmentation and custom regex rules to extract clause headings, then stored cleaned text in PostgreSQL.
2. **Vector indexing** – Embedded each clause with a fine‑tuned **BERT‑base** model (trained on a 5 k annotated clause dataset) and persisted vectors in **pgvector** for fast similarity search.
3. **Retrieval layer** – Implemented LangChain’s `VectorStoreRetriever` to fetch the top‑5 most relevant clauses for a user query.
4. **Generation layer** – Deployed a Hugging Face **DistilBERT‑QA** model, further fine‑tuned on the client’s risk‑labelled QA pairs, to synthesize a concise risk assessment.
5. **MLOps** – Containerized the entire stack with **Docker**, orchestrated via Docker‑Compose for local dev and later migrated to a Kubernetes cluster for production scaling. CI/CD pipelines (GitHub Actions) performed automated model validation and schema migrations.
6. **Evaluation** – Ran a hold‑out test set (1 k clauses) and measured precision/recall. Achieved **0.94 precision** and **0.88 recall** for risk identification.

## Stack
- **Language:** Python 3.10  
- **NLP Libraries:** Hugging Face Transformers, spaCy, LangChain  
- **Database:** PostgreSQL 13 with pgvector extension  
- **Containerization:** Docker 20.10, Docker‑Compose  
- **Version control & CI:** GitHub, GitHub Actions  
- **Deployment:** Kubernetes (EKS) for scaling the inference service  

## Results
| Metric | Target | Achieved |
|--------|--------|----------|
| Query turnaround time | < 1 s | **0.78 s** average (95 th percentile 0.92 s) |
| Risk identification precision | 0.94 | **0.94** |
| Contracts onboarded | — | **12 k** contracts (≈ 3 M clauses) indexed in 48 h |
| Lawyer satisfaction (survey) | — | **4.7/5** average rating for usefulness |

The bot was integrated into the client’s SaaS UI, reducing manual clause review time by **≈ 70 %** and enabling the legal team to flag high‑risk language within seconds of query.

## What I Would Do Differently
1. **Hybrid retrieval** – Combine dense vectors with BM25 sparse retrieval to improve recall on rare legal terminology.  
2. **Continuous learning loop** – Deploy an active‑learning UI where lawyers can correct model outputs, feeding those corrections back into periodic fine‑tuning.  
3. **Explainability** – Integrate LIME‑style token importance visualizations to increase trust in the generated risk explanations.  
4. **Multi‑language support** – Extend the pipeline to handle contracts in French and German, leveraging multilingual BERT models to broaden market reach.  

Overall, the project delivered a production‑ready RAG system that met the startup’s speed and accuracy goals while laying a solid foundation for future enhancements.
