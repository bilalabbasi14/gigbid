# Real‑Estate Document Analyzer – Phase 3 (2023, Real Estate)

When the client approached us in early 2023, they were struggling with a silent killer: model drift. Our document‑analysis pipeline, built on a fine‑tuned BERT model for extracting lease clauses and property descriptions, performed well in the lab, but once the system went live across their portfolio of 12,000 properties, the prediction distribution began to shift. Without any automated monitoring, the data science team was forced to run manual checks every quarter, often discovering performance degradation only after it had impacted downstream workflows.

As the MLOps Lead, I took ownership of designing a robust, production‑grade monitoring framework that could surface drift in near‑real time and trigger retraining only when truly needed. I started by containerizing the inference service with Docker, ensuring the same environment ran on both our on‑premise staging servers and the AWS SageMaker endpoints used for scaling. This gave us a single source of truth for dependencies—Python 3.10, transformers 4.31, and the custom tokenizers we’d built for the legal‑ese of real‑estate contracts.

The heart of the solution was an Airflow DAG that executed every 30 minutes. The DAG pulled a sample of 5,000 recent documents from the S3 bucket, ran them through the SageMaker endpoint, and logged the resulting prediction probabilities to MLflow. I configured MLflow to track both the model version and the distribution of the softmax scores, using a custom metric that computed the KL‑divergence between the current batch and a baseline captured during the last validated release.

When the divergence crossed the 5 % threshold—a value we arrived at after a two‑week A/B test comparing business impact of false positives versus false negatives—the DAG emitted a Slack alert to the data science lead and automatically queued a new training job on SageMaker. The job pulled the latest labeled contracts from the client’s internal data lake, retrained the BERT model with a learning rate schedule tuned in Optuna, and registered the new artifact in MLflow. Because the entire pipeline was orchestrated by Airflow, the retraining cycle could happen “as needed” rather than on a fixed quarterly cadence.

Over the six months following deployment, we saw the retraining cycles drop from four per year to just the two instances triggered by genuine drift. More importantly, the model’s F1 score held steady above 0.95, a significant improvement over the 0.89 we were seeing before monitoring was in place. The client reported a 20 % reduction in manual review time for lease extraction, translating to roughly $150 k in annual savings.

Beyond the immediate metrics, the project gave the client confidence that their AI stack could evolve with market changes—new lease formats, emerging property‑type vocabularies—without the risk of silent performance decay. The combination of Docker for reproducibility, SageMaker for scalable inference, Airflow for reliable orchestration, and MLflow for transparent experiment tracking proved to be a pragmatic, low‑overhead solution that met the enterprise’s stringent compliance and uptime requirements.
