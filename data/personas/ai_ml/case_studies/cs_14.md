# Sentiment‑aware Chatbot for Customer Support (2024, e‑commerce)

When the agency approached me in early 2024, their e‑commerce clients were drowning in a flood of support tickets, many of them charged with frustration or anger. The frontline agents were spending upwards of 70 % of their time triaging emotional tickets, which left routine inquiries languishing and drove the overall backlog to an unsustainable level. My mandate as the AI Engineer was to design a system that could not only answer common questions automatically but also detect sentiment in real time and route the most volatile conversations to a human operator.

I began by profiling the historical ticket data, which spanned roughly 150 k interactions from the previous year. Using Python and the Hugging Face Transformers library, I fine‑tuned a distilled‑BERT model on a balanced subset of 12 k manually labeled messages (positive, neutral, negative). The model achieved an F1‑score of 0.91 on a held‑out validation set, which gave me confidence that sentiment could be detected with low latency. To keep the inference pipeline lightweight for production, I exported the model to ONNX and wrapped it in a FastAPI service, exposing a `/sentiment` endpoint that responded in under 40 ms on a single‑core CPU instance.

For the dialogue management, I leveraged LangChain to orchestrate the flow between intent classification, knowledge‑base retrieval, and sentiment handling. Routine queries—order status, shipping estimates, return policies—were answered by pulling snippets from the client’s existing FAQ corpus using a vector store powered by FAISS. When the sentiment model flagged a message as “negative” with a confidence above 0.85, the LangChain chain triggered an escalation sub‑routine: the conversation was handed off to a live agent, the ticket was flagged in the CRM, and the user received a calming message acknowledging their frustration.

All components were containerized with Docker, enabling a reproducible CI/CD pipeline. I set up a GitLab CI job that built the images, ran unit and integration tests, and pushed the containers to the agency’s private registry. The deployment ran on a Kubernetes cluster with auto‑scaling enabled, ensuring that peak traffic spikes during holiday sales did not degrade response times.

After a six‑week sprint, we rolled the chatbot out to a pilot group covering 30 % of the client’s traffic. Within two weeks, the ticket backlog shrank by 43 %, and the customer satisfaction score—measured via post‑interaction surveys—climbed to 4.8 / 5, up from 3.9 / 5 before the launch. The sentiment‑aware routing proved especially valuable; angry customers who were promptly connected to a human agent reported a 67 % higher satisfaction rate than those who remained in the automated flow.

The project demonstrated how a tightly integrated NLP stack—combining sentiment detection, retrieval‑augmented generation, and robust MLOps practices—can transform a high‑volume support operation into a more humane, efficient experience for both customers and agents.
