# Loan Default Risk Scoring System

In early 2023, I was engaged by a mid-tier enterprise fintech firm to address a critical gap in their lending operations. The core problem was straightforward but impactful: the team lacked a robust statistical framework to predict which loan applicants would default within the first six months. Relying on manual underwriting heuristics, they were struggling to balance risk exposure with growth targets, often resulting in either overly conservative rejections or missed opportunities for high-quality borrowers. My role as the Data Scientist was to construct a data-driven alternative that could be validated against their existing processes.

The project lasted eight weeks and required working directly with historical loan data housed in Snowflake. I began by extracting and cleaning the dataset using `snowflake-connector-python` and `pandas`. The initial dataset was messy, with missing values in key behavioral metrics and inconsistent formatting in financial fields. I spent the first two weeks ensuring data integrity, imputing missing values where statistically justifiable and dropping records with fatal data gaps. Once the foundation was clean, I selected 25 financial and behavioral variables that showed strong correlation with default events. These included debt-to-income ratios, credit utilization history, employment stability indicators, and recent transaction patterns. I used `statsmodels` to perform exploratory analysis, checking for multicollinearity and ensuring the variables met the assumptions for linear modeling.

For the modeling phase, I opted for logistic regression rather than more complex machine learning algorithms. Given the need for interpretability in a regulated financial environment, a transparent model was essential. I built the model using `scikit-learn`, tuning hyperparameters to maximize the area under the ROC curve while maintaining a manageable number of non-zero coefficients. The model achieved a strong AUC of 0.84 on the validation set, indicating its ability to effectively distinguish between defaulters and non-defaulters.

The most challenging part of the project was validation. I designed an A/B testing framework to compare the new model’s recommendations against the legacy manual underwriting process. We ran the test in parallel for four weeks, routing 50% of new applicants through the model-driven scoring system and the other 50% through the traditional manual review. This approach allowed us to measure real-world impact without disrupting operations. The results were promising: the model-driven group showed a reduction in early-stage delinquencies, while the overall approval rate variance remained within 1.5% of the baseline, ensuring we did not unfairly restrict access to credit for qualified individuals.

By the end of the engagement, the integration of this scoring system had begun to show tangible benefits. The client reported an 8% year-over-year reduction in bad debt, a significant improvement for a company of their size. The project not only provided a tool for risk assessment but also established a new standard for data-driven decision-making within their lending team. I documented the entire process, from data preprocessing to model deployment, in a GitHub repository to ensure reproducibility and ease of handoff to their internal engineering team. The experience reinforced the value of combining statistical rigor with practical business constraints in fintech applications.
