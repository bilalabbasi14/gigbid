# Student Retention & Academic Performance Modeling: Enterprise Education Case Study (2021)

## Context
In 2021, a large enterprise university sought to address rising dropout rates among first-year students. The institution operates on a complex Learning Management System (LMS) that generates vast amounts of unstructured and semi-structured data, including login frequency, assignment submission timestamps, and forum participation. Despite having access to this data, the academic affairs team lacked the ability to translate raw metrics into actionable insights for individual student advisors. The goal was to move from reactive intervention—contacting students only after they had failed initial exams—to proactive retention strategies.

## Challenge
The primary challenge was data fragmentation and latency. Grade records were stored in a legacy SQL database, while behavioral LMS data resided in separate CSV exports downloaded weekly. There was no unified view of student performance. Furthermore, the university needed a solution that could be deployed quickly within a six-week timeline to impact the upcoming semester. The team needed to identify leading indicators of failure without requiring advanced machine learning expertise from the non-technical advising staff who would ultimately use the tool.

## Approach
I began by conducting a comprehensive data audit, cleaning and normalizing 18 months of historical data using Python and pandas. I engineered features such as "days since last login," "assignment completion variance," and "peer interaction frequency." Using scikit-learn, I trained a Random Forest classifier to predict semester failure, achieving a balanced accuracy of 82% on the validation set. I focused particularly on precision to minimize false positives, as incorrectly flagging high-performing students would waste advising resources.

To bridge the gap between the model and the end-users, I developed an automated pipeline using SQLAlchemy to extract fresh data weekly. The prediction scores were then written to a structured Excel workbook via openpyxl. This workbook included conditional formatting to highlight high-risk students and a lookup table mapping risk factors to specific tutoring resources and counseling services. This allowed advisors to open a single file and immediately see who needed help and why.

## Stack
*   **Python:** Core data manipulation and modeling.
*   **pandas:** Data cleaning, feature engineering, and aggregation.
*   **scikit-learn:** Random Forest classification and model evaluation.
*   **SQLAlchemy:** Database connectivity and query execution.
*   **openpyxl:** Programmatic generation of Excel reports with formatting.
*   **Excel:** End-user interface for student advisors.

## Results
The deployment coincided with the start of the spring 2021 semester. By week 4, the model successfully identified 85% of students who ultimately failed the course by the end of the term. More importantly, the intervention program driven by these flags increased the retention rate for flagged students by 9% compared to the previous year’s cohort. Advisors reported that the Excel tool reduced the time spent triaging student lists by approximately 4 hours per week, allowing them to focus on direct outreach.

## What I would do differently for this project
While the Excel-based solution was effective for the immediate timeline, it lacked scalability. For a future iteration, I would have built a lightweight web dashboard using Streamlit or integrated the results into the university’s existing LMS interface. This would have eliminated the manual download and upload process, ensuring real-time updates. Additionally, I would have engaged the advising staff earlier in the feature engineering phase to ensure the risk factors aligned with their pedagogical understanding of student behavior, potentially improving model interpretability and buy-in.
