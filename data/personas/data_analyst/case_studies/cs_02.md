# Subscription Revenue Attribution & LTV Modeling for a SaaS Startup (2024)

In early 2024, I was engaged as an independent consultant by a Series B SaaS startup that was struggling with a critical blind spot in their growth strategy: they could not accurately attribute subscription revenue to specific acquisition channels over time. The leadership team believed their marketing spend was yielding diminishing returns, but internal reporting suggested otherwise. This discrepancy stemmed from a fragmented data stack where revenue events in BigQuery were not reliably linked to first-touch or multi-touch attribution signals from Google Analytics 4. The result was a misallocation of budget, with significant funds flowing into low-performing channels that appeared healthy only because of flawed first-touch logic.

My primary objective was to rebuild the revenue mart to support accurate, dynamic lifecycle value (LTV) modeling and channel attribution. I began by auditing the existing SQL models in their dbt project, which were heavily reliant on hard-coded lookback windows that failed to capture the true journey of a B2B buyer. I rewrote the intermediate models using dbt-core, introducing a robust multi-touch attribution logic that assigned credit based on geometric decay weighting rather than simple first-or-last touch. This required complex window functions in BigQuery to handle sessions that spanned multiple weeks and distinct user identifiers that shifted between anonymous and authenticated states.

To operationalize this, I developed a new set of dbt models that joined marketing spend data with downstream revenue events, ensuring that every dollar spent could be traced to a specific cohort’s eventual lifetime value. The critical innovation, however, was in the presentation layer. I built a Power BI dashboard that didn’t just display historical ROAS but projected future LTV using a custom DAX measure. This measure dynamically adjusted based on cohort retention curves, allowing stakeholders to see not just where a customer came from, but how much value that specific channel was likely to generate over the next 12 months. I used Python and pandas to pre-process raw GA4 logs for outlier detection before ingestion, ensuring that bot traffic or test accounts didn’t skew the statistical models.

The project lasted five weeks, during which we iterated on the attribution weights to match the sales team’s qualitative feedback. By the end of the engagement, we had identified a 12% over-allocation to two low-performing paid social channels that had been masking their inefficiency due to high immediate conversion rates but poor long-term retention. Conversely, organic search and content marketing channels, previously undervalued, were shown to drive higher-quality cohorts with stronger LTV trajectories. This shift in perspective improved the accuracy of their ROAS reporting by 25%, as the new model accounted for delayed revenue recognition inherent in SaaS sales cycles.

We documented the entire data lineage and model logic in a public repository, `saas-ltv-attribution-model`, to ensure transparency and ease of handover to their internal data engineering team. The client was able to reallocate approximately $15,000 monthly in ad spend immediately upon receiving the final report, pausing underperforming campaigns and doubling down on high-LTV channels. This engagement highlighted the importance of moving beyond simple attribution models to a more nuanced, statistical approach that respects the time lag between acquisition and monetization in the SaaS industry.
