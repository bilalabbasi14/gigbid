# Case Study: Supply Chain Delay Prediction & Route Optimization (2024, Logistics)

## Context
In 2024, I was engaged by a major enterprise logistics firm operating across North America to address a critical operational bottleneck. The client manages over 15,000 daily shipments, a volume that made manual monitoring of delivery windows impossible. At the time of engagement, their on-time performance (OTP) rate had stagnated at approximately 86%, with a significant portion of late deliveries attributed to unpredictable traffic congestion and severe weather events that disrupted standard routing algorithms.

## Challenge
The core problem was the inability to proactively identify shipments at high risk of delay until the driver was already en route. The client’s existing monitoring systems were reactive, relying on real-time GPS data to alert dispatchers only after a deviation occurred. This lack of forward-looking insight resulted in missed SLA deadlines, eroding customer satisfaction scores and incurring contractual penalties. The specific challenge was to build a predictive capability that could flag high-risk shipments at least 24 hours before the scheduled delivery window, providing dispatchers with actionable time to reroute vehicles or proactively notify clients.

## Approach
I led the analytical effort over a 10-week period, focusing on transforming raw telemetry into predictive intelligence. The process began with a comprehensive data audit of three years of historical GPS coordinates and delivery timestamps stored in BigQuery. I utilized Python and pandas to clean and feature-engineer this massive dataset, isolating key variables such as historical traffic density by region, weather API data, and driver-specific performance metrics.

Using scipy and statistical modeling techniques, I identified that 70% of delays were correlated with specific weather-traffic interaction patterns rather than mechanical failures. I then developed a classification model to predict the probability of a shipment arriving late. To operationalize this, I designed an Apache Airflow DAG that ingested nightly data, ran the prediction model, and output a risk-scored list of shipments to the dispatch dashboard. This allowed the client to shift from reactive tracking to proactive route optimization.

## Stack
*   **Data Warehouse:** Google BigQuery
*   **Orchestration:** Apache Airflow
*   **Analysis & Modeling:** Python, pandas, scipy
*   **Visualization:** matplotlib, Power BI (for dispatcher interface)
*   **Cloud SDK:** google-cloud-bigquery

## Results
The implementation of the predictive delay model yielded immediate and measurable improvements in operational efficiency. By enabling dispatchers to reroute high-risk shipments before congestion set in, the firm reduced late deliveries by 18%. Consequently, the overall on-time performance metric improved to 94%, a significant jump from the previous baseline. The project also provided a reusable framework for analyzing other logistical variables, contributing to a more resilient supply chain strategy.

## What I would do differently for this project
While the 24-hour prediction window was effective, I would now prioritize a more granular time-series approach for the first 6 hours of transit. In hindsight, incorporating real-time IoT sensor data from the vehicles themselves could have reduced the false-positive rate for weather-related delays. Additionally, I would have invested more time in the initial phase in stakeholder workshops with the drivers themselves. Their qualitative feedback on "blind spots" in certain urban areas provided invaluable context that pure GPS data missed. Integrating this domain knowledge earlier could have accelerated the feature engineering process and potentially improved the model's accuracy for the most complex urban routes.
