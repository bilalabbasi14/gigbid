# Engineering Log: Hotel Occupancy & RevPAR Analytics (Hospitality, 2021)

*   **Project ID:** p06
*   **Role:** Freelance Analyst
*   **Duration:** 3 Weeks (Q3 2021)
*   **Stack:** Power BI Desktop, DAX, Excel, pandas, openpyxl

## Context & Problem Statement
Client is an independent hotel manager operating a single property. The core operational bottleneck was the inability to distinguish between organic seasonal demand spikes and the actual effectiveness of pricing strategy adjustments. Previous manual reporting in Excel was error-prone, took approximately 2 days per cycle, and lacked the granularity to correlate Average Daily Rate (ADR) changes with occupancy levels. The primary objective was to consolidate Property Management System (PMS) data into a dynamic analytics environment to isolate pricing impacts from seasonal noise.

## Data Ingestion & Preprocessing
*   **Source Data:** Extracted raw transaction logs from the PMS via CSV export. Data spanned 24 months (2019-2020) to establish seasonal baselines.
*   **Preprocessing Pipeline:**
    *   Used `pandas` in Python for initial data cleaning.
    *   Standardized date formats (`YYYY-MM-DD`) and currency values.
    *   Handled missing values in `occupancy_rate` by forward-filling within 7-day windows; dropped rows with null `room_type`.
    *   Transformed raw nightly rates into `revenue` and `rooms_sold` columns.
*   **Validation:** Cross-checked total revenue figures against the PMS monthly summary report. Discrepancy found in 0.3% of records due to double-bookings; flagged and excluded these `transaction_ids` from the final dataset.
*   **Tooling:** `openpyxl` used to parse complex Excel workbooks containing historical rate cards from previous management teams.

## Power BI Model & DAX Development
*   **Data Model:** Created a star schema with `Fact_Reservations` and dimensions for `Date`, `Room_Type`, and `Market_Segment`.
*   **Key DAX Measures:**
    *   `Total Revenue`: `SUM(Fact_Reservations[revenue])`
    *   `Total Rooms Available`: `COUNTROWS(Fact_Reservations) * RELATED(Dim_Date[days_in_month])`
    *   `ADR`: `DIVIDE([Total Revenue], [Rooms Sold], 0)`
    *   `RevPAR`: `DIVIDE([Total Revenue], [Total Rooms Available], 0)`
    *   `Occupancy %`: `DIVIDE([Rooms Sold], [Total Rooms Available], 0)`
*   **Dynamic Analysis:** Implemented `CALCULATE` and `TIME INTELLIGENCE` functions (`SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR`, `TOTALYTD`) to enable year-over-year and year-to-date comparisons.
*   **Optimization:** Optimized the model by removing unused columns from the `Fact_Reservations` table, reducing dataset size by 15% and improving refresh times from 45s to 12s.

## Visualization & Reporting
*   **Report Structure:** Three-page layout:
    1.  **Executive Summary:** KPI cards for current month RevPAR, ADR, and Occupancy, with MoM variance indicators.
    2.  **Trend Analysis:** Line charts displaying 12-month trends for ADR and RevPAR, with a slicer for `Season` (Peak, Shoulder, Off-Peak).
    3.  **Drill-Down:** Matrix visual allowing the manager to drill from property level to specific `Room_Type` and `Market_Segment`, highlighting which segments drove revenue growth.
*   **Interactivity:** Added tooltips to display detailed breakdowns of revenue by booking channel.

## Outcomes & Delivery
*   **Performance Improvement:** The manager identified that increasing ADR by 5% during shoulder seasons did not negatively impact occupancy, validating a more aggressive dynamic pricing strategy.
*   **Efficiency Gain:** Reporting preparation time reduced from 2 days (48 hours) to 2 hours (12 hours) due to automated data refresh and pre-built DAX logic.
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   Power BI Desktop file (`Hotel_Analytics.pbix`).
    *   Data dictionary documenting all DAX measures and data sources.
    *   User guide for navigating the drill-down report.

## Notes & Trade-offs
*   **Limitation:** The model relies on monthly CSV exports from the PMS. A direct API connection was not feasible within the 3-week timeline and budget constraints.
*   **Future Work:** Recommend integrating a third-party demand forecasting tool to predict future occupancy based on local event calendars.
